Highlights

  • Ontario: every organization said yes to working together; Indigenous grandmothers caring alone and a French-language gap were the clearest needs identified.
  • Time to Attach: federal announcement tentatively set for Dec 2026 (confidential until confirmed); ESDC had been hopeful for year-end implementation.
  • Provinces: Ontario and Manitoba are ready to roll out Time to Attach (legislation must still be revisited to include kinship and customary caregivers); Alberta, Saskatchewan and Nova Scotia labour ministries briefed; PEI booked Nov 9.
  • AFCCA: Alberta Family Ministry very interested; Newfoundland & Labrador asked the MCCSS Child Welfare Secretariat to invite Interwoven to present to all provincial Deputy Ministers and ADMs.
  • MCCSS funding: early talks to continue the kinship program past the March 2027 pilot end; all proposals to be submitted by end of November.

The big picture

  • New partnerships: every organization visited said yes to working together.
  • Sharing programs: tools and training for workers supporting children with trauma and families in adoption or kinship care.

  • Fall events: several organizations want Interwoven at September Kinship Awareness Week events.

Dr. Emmanuel ChilangaUNBC School of Social Work · Associate Professor

PhD and MSW (McGill), MSc Geography (Western), BEd (Malawi). Interdisciplinary scholarship in critical, community-engaged and green social work: critical social policy, Africentric and decolonial practice, refugee governance and forced migration, Indigenous child and family welfare, and northern, rural and remote social work.

His research examines how law, policy, colonialism, racism, environmental change and institutions shape health, livelihoods, rights and resilience. Current work: refugee protection and the right to work in Uganda and Malawi; Indigenous child welfare and water governance in northern BC; anti-Black racism and community development; food and nutrition security; maternal and child health; social work and field education.

Methods: qualitative, mixed-methods, participatory, critical policy analysis and community-based. Works with Indigenous Nations, refugee-led and community organizations, agencies, universities and public institutions in Canada and Africa. Teaching is grounded in social justice, environmental justice, human rights and cultural responsiveness.
